# Hospital-Emergency-Room-Dashboard
A data analytics project focused on analyzing Hospital Emergency Room patient data using an interactive dashboard. The project explores patient admission status, waiting time, satisfaction scores, gender, age groups, and department referrals. I'm continuing to improve my skills in Excel, SQL, Power BI, data visualization, through projects like this
📊 Project Overview
I recently worked on a Hospital Emergency Room Dashboard to learn how to analyze healthcare data and turn raw patient information into meaningful insights.

The dashboard focuses on important Emergency Room metrics such as:

👥 Total number of patients

🏥 Patient admission status

⏱️ Average waiting time

⭐ Patient satisfaction score

👩‍⚕️ Gender-wise analysis

🎂 Patient age groups

🏨 Department referrals

🎯 Key Analysis: Patient Admission Status
One of the main questions I wanted to answer was:

How many patients were admitted to the hospital compared with those who were not admitted?

Based on the dashboard:

Patient Admission Status
Distribution of 479 emergency room patients by admission status.

Not Admitted (50.5%)
Admitted (49.5%)
Admission Status	Patients	Percentage
Not Admitted	242	51%
Admitted	237	49%
Total	479	100%

🔎 What I Learned From the Analysis
The dashboard shows that out of 479 patients:

242 patients (51%) were not admitted

237 patients (49%) were admitted

The difference between the two groups is 5 patients

The admission and non-admission percentages are therefore quite close to each other.

This analysis helped me understand how a simple admission-status field can be transformed into a useful KPI for monitoring Emergency Room activity.

📈 Other Dashboard Insights
The dashboard also helped me explore:

Average Wait Time: 34.90

Patient Satisfaction Score: 5.30

Age Group Analysis:
The dashboard breaks patients into age groups from 0–09 through 70–79, making it easier to understand the distribution of Emergency Room patients by age.

Department Referrals:
The largest category shown is None (263 patients), followed by General Practice (115) and Orthopedics (45).
